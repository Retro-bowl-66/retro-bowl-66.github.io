Inside 2048: The sliding number puzzle everyone else keeps copying

Gabriele Cirulli's puzzle arrived in March 2014 and quietly ate a week of the internet's productivity. Its rules barely fill a sentence. Shove numbered tiles around a sixteen-cell board, combine equal values into their sum, and keep at it until 2048 appears or the board jams.

Gameplay overview

One swipe sends the entire board sliding, and twin values that meet become a single tile worth their total. A fresh low number then lands somewhere empty. Hitting 2048 counts as victory, although nothing stops you from carrying on. Defeat comes when every cell is occupied and no pair can combine.

Beginner's checklist

  • Park your biggest tile in a corner and guard that spot.
  • Lean on two directions most of the time, using a third sparingly.
  • Keep the edge row beside your big tile filled so nothing shifts it.
  • Clean up small tiles early before they spread everywhere.

Control scheme

  • Arrow / WASD Keys – move Number Tiles

Strategy notes

The classic approach is a descending staircase along one edge: the biggest value tucked in the corner, smaller ones lined up beside it in order, so every combination feeds the next. The risky move is any swipe that opens a gap next to the corner tile, because a stray low number can slip in and wreck the chain.

Final verdict

Few puzzles achieve this much with so little, and the core idea remains satisfying years later. Once you learn the corner strategy, though, runs can feel procedural rather than clever. Still the version to play before trying any of its many remixes.
