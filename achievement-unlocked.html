Inside Achievement Unlocked: A hundred awards for doing almost anything

John Cooney's 2008 Flash game is a joke about video game rewards that also happens to be a decent puzzle. Your avatar is a little elephant stuck on a single screen, and the only goal is to collect achievements. There are one hundred of them. Some pop for walking left. Others demand strange, deliberate behavior the game never explains.

Gameplay overview

Movement uses only the arrow keys, and the elephant can walk, jump and bump into the platforms and hazards that make up the level. Almost any action might trigger a new award, announced with a satisfying banner. A list shows which achievements remain, often with cryptic names hinting at what they want. The challenge is working out what counts.

Beginner's checklist

  • Collect the easy ones first by moving and jumping in every direction.
  • Read the achievement names closely, since many describe the action they want.
  • Try silly things on purpose, such as standing still or repeating an action.
  • Do not be precious about your elephant; losing a life can count for something too.

Control scheme

  • Arrow Keys – move

Strategy notes

Work methodically once the obvious awards are done. Sort the remaining names into ones you understand and ones you do not, tackle the clear ones, then experiment around the level's features for the rest. Some achievements reward patience or repetition, so a stubborn one may just need doing many more times than seems sensible.

Final verdict

Funny, self-aware and surprisingly compelling, it pokes fun at reward loops while running one on you. The final stretch of achievements can be frustratingly obscure without a hint. A clever curiosity from the Flash era that still earns a short session.
