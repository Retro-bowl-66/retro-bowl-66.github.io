Editorial Policy

Every game on Retro-Bowl-66 has a written guide. This page explains how those guides are structured, how we reach our verdicts and what happens when we get something wrong.

How a guide is built

Each guide follows the same sections, so you always know where to look.

  • Gameplay overview: what you do in the game, what the goal is and how a typical session unfolds.
  • Beginner's checklist: three to five practical steps for your first few minutes.
  • Control scheme: the keys, mouse actions or touch input the game expects.
  • Strategy notes: deeper advice for once the basics are under control, such as priorities, common mistakes and ways to score higher.
  • Final verdict: a balanced judgment of what works, what does not and who is likely to enjoy it.
  • Details: the developer, genre, release information and category, where known.

How verdicts are formed

A verdict judges a game on its own terms. A short arcade game is measured by whether its core loop holds up, not by whether it has a story. We look at how quickly the game makes sense, how responsive the controls feel, how much variety it offers and whether it suits a short session. Real weaknesses, such as repetition, unclear menus or clumsy touch controls, get named even in games we like. We do not use numeric scores, because a number tends to hide exactly the trade-offs a reader needs to know about.

Fact-checking

Before a guide goes live, we check its controls, its stated goal and its details box against the game itself and against information the developer has published. When we cannot confirm a fact, such as a release date or a developer name, we leave it out rather than guess.

Corrections

Games get updated, and guides can fall behind. If you find an error, email contact@retro-bowl-66.github.io with the page address and what needs fixing. We correct factual mistakes and revise guides when a game changes in a way that affects the advice. Small fixes are made directly; larger changes to a verdict are made only after the game has been looked at again.

Independence from advertisers

The site is funded by ads, but advertisers have no say in which games we list or what the guides say about them. We do not accept payment for reviews or for better placement in the library, and no developer can buy a kinder verdict.
