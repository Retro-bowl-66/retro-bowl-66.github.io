Inside Elastic Face: A rubbery face you can pull in every direction

David Li's 2021 WebGL toy is not really a game, and it does not pretend to be. A face appears on the screen. You grab it. It stretches like warm rubber and springs back the moment you let go. There is no score, no goal and no way to fail, only a surprisingly convincing piece of physics and the urge to pull a little further.

Gameplay overview

Grab any spot and drag, and the skin follows your cursor, deforming smoothly before springing home. Cheeks, nose, chin and brow all respond, so you can tug the face into grotesque masks or gentle stretches. The simulation reacts in real time, which is where the odd satisfaction comes from. Nothing you do can break it.

Beginner's checklist

  • Pull slowly to watch how the surface deforms.
  • Release suddenly to see the snap-back motion.
  • Try stretching two areas one after the other to make strange expressions.

Strategy notes

There is nothing to optimize, so the only strategy is play. Treat it as a digital stress toy: a minute of stretching can be oddly relaxing after a long stretch of reading. Experiment with fast and slow pulls to see how the physics responds. Then close it, because that is about all it offers.

Final verdict

Impressive as a tech demo and briefly entertaining as a toy. With no goals at all, it holds attention for a few minutes at most. Worth a look for the novelty, not for lasting play.
