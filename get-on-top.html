Inside Get On Top: Two players, clumsy limbs and one shared goal

Clumsy control as comedy is Bennett Foddy's signature, and this 2012 duel shows it off as well as anything he has made. A pair of grapplers are locked in a clinch, each straining to tip the other over. The keys behave awkwardly by design, and the tangle that follows is as entertaining for spectators as for the two people pressing buttons.

Gameplay overview

Player one uses WASD and player two the arrow keys, with each key controlling part of the fighter's body rather than simple movement. The aim is to force your opponent's head to touch the ground. Because nobody has precise control, rounds become wobbly tugs of war full of near misses and sudden collapses.

Beginner's checklist

  • Tap each key in turn to find out which limb it drives.
  • Use small, careful presses instead of hammering every key.
  • Protect your own head while steering theirs toward the mat.

Control scheme

  • WASD Keys – move (Player 1)
  • Arrow Keys – move (Player 2)

Strategy notes

Leverage beats aggression. Small shifts in balance can tip your opponent over, while frantic inputs often topple you instead. Watch how your opponent leans and push in the same direction to exaggerate it. Most rounds are won by the player who stays calmer, not the one who presses more keys.

Final verdict

Hysterical with a friend and a model of the clumsy-controls style Foddy is known for. It needs a second person to make any sense. One of the best party duels around.
