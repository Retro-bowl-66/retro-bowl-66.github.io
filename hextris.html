Inside Hextris: Spin a hexagon to catch blocks by color

Logan Engstrom's 2014 puzzle takes the falling-block idea and wraps it around a shape. A hexagon sits in the middle of the screen, and colored blocks slide toward it from all six sides. You cannot move the blocks. Instead you rotate the hexagon so each one lands where it can join others of the same color.

Gameplay overview

Turning the hexagon left or right decides which side catches each incoming block. When three or more blocks of one color touch, they clear and score points. Uncleared blocks stack outward, and if any side grows too tall, the game ends. The pace increases steadily, so rotations must become quicker and more decisive.

Beginner's checklist

  • Rotate early, before blocks get close to the hexagon.
  • Group colors on specific sides to make matches easier.
  • Watch all six sides, not only the one you are focused on.

Strategy notes

Planning which sides collect which colors keeps the board manageable. Avoid stacking mixed colors on one side, since they block future matches. As speed rises, think in terms of rotation count rather than angles, because one or two turns usually covers any direction. Big combos relieve pressure on several sides at once.

Final verdict

Clever, elegant and genuinely addictive, it is one of the smarter twists on classic block puzzles. The pace can become overwhelming, and the minimalist look is plain. A strong choice for fans of quick puzzle games.
