Minesweeper Online

Inside Minesweeper: Pure deduction with a small chance of disaster

Few games have shipped on as many computers as this one, and fewer still have taught so many people to think in probabilities without noticing. Alister Scott's browser version leaves the rules untouched: uncover every safe square on the grid without setting off a single hidden mine.

Gameplay overview

Clicking a square reveals it. Empty squares open up their neighbors automatically, while a numbered square tells you how many of the eight cells around it are mined. From those clues you deduce which hidden squares are safe, marking suspected mines with flags. Clear every safe square to win; reveal a mine and the round ends on the spot.

Beginner's checklist

  • Begin on the smallest board until reading the numbers feels automatic.
  • Flag a mine only when the numbers prove it, not when it merely feels likely.
  • Learn the 1-2-1 pattern along a wall, since it pins down two mines at once.

Strategy notes

Most of the game is pattern recognition. A 1-1 pair starting at an edge tells you the third square along is safe, and a 1-2-1 means the mines sit beneath the ones. When pure logic runs out, guess where the odds are best and where a safe result would open new information. Speed comes later; accuracy builds it.

Final verdict

It remains one of the cleanest logic puzzles around, and this version is faithful. The occasional forced guess still stings, and the presentation is plain. Careful thinkers will get great value, and one beginner board fits neatly into a short pause between classes.
