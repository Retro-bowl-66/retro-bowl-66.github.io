Inside Mini-putt: Tiny greens, tricky angles, plenty of geometry

Miniature golf translates to a browser almost perfectly. There is no wind, no club selection and no wobbly swing, just a ball, a cup and whatever the course designer placed in between. This Flash-era game treats each hole as a compact puzzle of walls, ramps and obstacles.

Gameplay overview

Every shot follows the same rhythm. You aim, choose how much power to put behind the ball and let it go. The ball rolls, bounces off rails and reacts to slopes, and the best routes often involve a deliberate rebound rather than a straight line. Fewer strokes is always the target, so one sloppy hole can undo several good ones.

Beginner's checklist

  • Study the whole hole before your first shot and trace where the ball could rebound.
  • Use less power than you think, because overhit balls skip right past the cup.
  • Settle for a good second shot when a hole-in-one route looks too risky.

Strategy notes

Bank shots are the heart of it. A ball leaves a wall at roughly the angle it arrived, so you can find a rebound line by imagining the cup mirrored behind the wall. Power control matters just as much: a slow ball near the hole can drop, while a fast one rattles away. Play safe on awkward holes and take risks only on short ones.

Final verdict

Mini-putt is charming and easy to pick up, and the bank-shot puzzles are satisfying when they click. Holes start to feel similar after a while, and the presentation shows its age. A good choice for relaxed players who enjoy a little geometry.
