Inside Money Movers 3: A third round of jailhouse teamwork

Series fatigue is a fair worry by part three. Meow Beast answers it less with novelty than with denser puzzles, packing switches, doors and guards into rooms that need both brothers working in step. Most of the tools will look familiar to returning pairs; the challenge comes from how tightly they are combined.

Gameplay overview

Each level remains a self-contained puzzle for two players on one keyboard. Switches open doors, guards patrol set routes and obstacles block the direct path, so success depends on dividing the work and moving at the right moments. Later stages ask for longer chains of actions, where one early slip can unravel an entire plan.

Beginner's checklist

  • Play the earlier games first if you can, since this one assumes you know the basics.
  • Keep both brothers out of guard sight lines while one of them works a switch.
  • Note which switches control which doors before anyone commits to a path.

Control scheme

  • Arrow Keys – move (Player 1)
  • WASD Keys – move (Player 2)

Strategy notes

Long puzzles reward a planning pause. Before moving, trace the order of switches and doors backward from the exit; it often reveals which brother needs to be where at the end. Avoid splitting far apart unless the level requires it, because regrouping near guards costs time. If you fail at the same point twice, the real problem usually sits earlier in the sequence.

Final verdict

Money Movers 3 is a satisfying third chapter and the most demanding of the trio. It offers few surprises, and newcomers should begin with the first game. For practiced duos, it is a solid test of communication.
