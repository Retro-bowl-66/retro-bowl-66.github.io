Inside Retro Bowl: Quarterback on Sunday, general manager all week

Pixel football rarely looks this simple or plays this deep. New Star Games' Retro Bowl, released in January 2020, makes you both the quarterback on the field and the coach in the front office. You throw every pass yourself, then spend the week between games juggling contracts, morale and roster spots. Its popularity is easy to explain once you play a season.

Gameplay overview

On offense, you drag to aim a pass, release to throw and then control the receiver or running back after the catch. Defense plays out automatically, so your offensive drives decide most games. Off the field, you sign and release players, manage a salary cap, upgrade facilities and keep the locker room happy. The goal is to build a dynasty and win the Retro Bowl.

Beginner's checklist

  • Throw to open receivers rather than forcing passes into coverage.
  • Keep player morale high, since unhappy stars can disrupt the team.
  • Spend coaching credits wisely on upgrades and key players.
  • Watch the salary cap before signing expensive free agents.

Strategy notes

Your offense carries the team, so efficient drives matter more than flashy plays. Short, safe passes keep the chains moving and limit turnovers, while deep throws are best saved for open looks. Off the field, invest in a few elite players rather than many average ones, and keep morale up through wins and smart roster choices. Facility upgrades pay off over several seasons.

Final verdict

Retro Bowl is a brilliant mix of arcade football and team management, with charming visuals and real depth. The automatic defense can feel frustrating, and seasons get repetitive over time. Anyone who likes sports games, football fan or not, should try it.
