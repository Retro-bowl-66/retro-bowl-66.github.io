Inside Solitaire: The card game that taught millions to use a mouse

Klondike needs one player, one deck and a little optimism. This HTML5 edition lays out the familiar seven piles, with only the bottom card of each turned up, plus four empty slots waiting for aces and a face-down reserve in the corner. It remains one of the gentlest ways to fill a short study break without getting sucked into something longer.

Gameplay overview

On the table, you build downward with colors alternating, so a red nine sits on a black ten and a black eight follows. When the table runs dry, you flip through the reserve for something playable. Each foundation slot starts with an ace and climbs by suit through to the king. Get all fifty-two cards up there and the deal is yours.

Beginner's checklist

  • Uncover face-down cards in the piles before leaning on the reserve.
  • Send aces and twos up to the foundations the moment they surface.
  • Put only a king into an empty pile, ideally one that frees other cards.

Strategy notes

Revealing hidden cards beats almost every other consideration. Given two legal moves, choose the one that turns something over, especially in the tallest piles. Be wary of rushing mid-value cards up to the foundations while they could still hold a sequence together on the table. Some deals are simply unwinnable, so the skill lies in squeezing the most from the ones that are not.

Final verdict

Solitaire is timeless, calm and endlessly replayable, and this version does the basics well. Luck still decides some deals, and the presentation is plain. Anyone who wants a quiet, thoughtful card game will feel at home.
