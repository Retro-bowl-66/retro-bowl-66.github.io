Sort The Court Online

Inside Sort The Court: Ruling a kingdom one yes or no at a time

Being a monarch turns out to be mostly paperwork. Graeme Borland's July 2017 game seats you on a small throne and sends a steady queue of petitioners your way, each with a request that you can only accept or refuse. That two-button design sounds trivial, yet it produces a charming, often funny little kingdom simulator.

Gameplay overview

Visitors arrive one after another asking for money, permission, favors or help with oddly specific problems. Your answer shifts three numbers: population, happiness and gold. Some characters return over several days, and earlier decisions can come back with unexpected consequences. The aim is to grow a thriving kingdom without letting any of the three values collapse.

Beginner's checklist

  • Keep an eye on all three numbers rather than only your treasury.
  • Remember recurring visitors, because their stories continue across days.
  • Say yes to spending only when you can afford the cost.

Strategy notes

Balance matters more than maximizing any single stat. Gold funds growth, happiness keeps people around and population drives everything else, so a decision that boosts one at a heavy cost to another rarely pays off. Pay attention to the tone of requests; suspicious offers often hide a catch. Replaying lets you see how different choices branch.

Final verdict

Sort The Court is a witty, inventive decision game with a lot of personality. Its simplicity limits depth, and repeat playthroughs reveal its patterns. Players who enjoy light humor and quick choices will be charmed.
