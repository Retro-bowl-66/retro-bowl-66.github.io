Inside Spider Solitaire: Two decks, ten columns and a lot of patience

Spider Solitaire is where Klondike players go once the original stops putting up a fight. It uses two full decks spread over ten piles, and the target is to assemble complete same-suit runs, king at the top and ace at the bottom, each of which vanishes from the table once finished. Learning takes minutes; mastering it can take months.

Gameplay overview

Any card can sit on one a rank higher, regardless of suit, but only an unbroken single-suit run can be lifted as a unit. That rule is what makes the game interesting, because mixed stacks quickly become dead weight. When you run out of useful moves, the reserve drops one fresh card onto every pile at once. Clear all one hundred and four cards to win.

Beginner's checklist

  • Prefer moves that build runs of one suit whenever there is a choice.
  • Work toward an empty pile, the most flexible space on the table.
  • Use up every helpful move before asking the reserve for more cards.

Strategy notes

Empty piles are precious. They let you park cards temporarily and untangle a messy stack, so freeing one should be an early priority. Mixed-suit stacks help in a pinch but turn into liabilities, so tidy them up when the chance appears. Before calling for more cards, flip over as many face-down cards as you can, since every new deal buries the table under another layer.

Final verdict

Spider Solitaire is a deeper, more strategic cousin of Klondike and wonderfully absorbing. Harder variants can feel brutal, and long games demand patience. Card game fans looking for a real challenge will love it.
