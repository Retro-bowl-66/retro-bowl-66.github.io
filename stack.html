Inside Stack: Timing a tower, one sliding slab at a time

Explaining Stack takes one breath. A slab glides across the top of a tower, you tap, and it stops. Whatever part of it is not resting on the slab underneath gets shaved away, so every slightly early or late tap leaves you a thinner platform to build on. Eventually the platform is a sliver, and then it is nothing.

Gameplay overview

Height is the only score. Each new slab enters from the side and sweeps back and forth until you drop it. Nail the alignment and the tower keeps its full footprint; miss by a little and the excess falls away. The slabs move faster the higher you climb, so concentration has to rise along with the tower.

Beginner's checklist

  • Watch a slab sweep across once before your very first tap.
  • Chase exact alignment early, while there is still width to protect.
  • Tap to a steady beat instead of speeding up when the slabs do.

Strategy notes

Rhythm beats reaction. Slabs travel at a predictable pace, so tap in time with them rather than waiting until the last instant. Fix your eyes on the edge of the slab below and drop when the moving one lines up. After a sloppy drop, the narrower tower demands more care, so slow your breathing and stay consistent. One tower is about the length of a short pause between readings.

Final verdict

Stack is a clean, hypnotic timing game with an instantly satisfying loop. It has almost no variety, and long sessions become repetitive. Players who enjoy minimalist challenges will happily chase a taller tower.
