Inside Tube Jumpers: Hopping to survive, ideally with friends

Michael Eichler's June 2016 Unity game asks for exactly one skill, and it asks for it constantly: hop at the right instant. Played solo, that makes for a neat little reflex drill. Add a couple of friends on the same keyboard and the mood changes completely, with yelling, groaning and accusations of sabotage.

Gameplay overview

Hazards keep approaching, and leaving the ground at the correct moment is the only defense. A mistimed hop knocks you out of the round. The tempo creeps upward the whole time, so the relaxed beat of the first few seconds turns hectic before long. Rounds are brief, and in a group, outlasting everyone else is what counts.

Beginner's checklist

  • Let two or three hazards pass to feel the beat before committing.
  • Lean toward hopping slightly early once the tempo climbs.
  • Round up a friend or two, because the game is far livelier that way.

Strategy notes

Rhythm beats reaction here. Count the gaps between hazards, then hop to that count rather than to what your eyes report at the last moment. When the tempo rises, keep the count and simply speed it up. With several players, ignore everyone else's character entirely, since glancing at a rival is the fastest way to miss your own cue.

Final verdict

Tube Jumpers is a simple arcade game that comes alive in company. On your own, it runs out of surprises fast. A good pick for a few rowdy rounds with friends.
