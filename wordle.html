Inside Wordle: Five letters, six guesses and a daily ritual

Josh Wardle launched Wordle in October 2021, and within a few months it had colonized group chats everywhere. The premise fits on a sticky note: one secret five-letter word, six attempts, and colored hints after each try. It is short, sociable and sized almost perfectly for a coffee run or the walk across campus between lectures.

Gameplay overview

Type in any real five-letter word and press enter. The tiles then flip: green says that letter is in exactly the right slot, yellow says it belongs elsewhere in the answer, and gray says forget it. Each round of hints shrinks the field of candidates. Pin down the answer by the sixth row and you have won the day.

Beginner's checklist

  • Open with a word stuffed with frequent letters, such as one using E, A, R and S.
  • Spend guess two on fresh letters rather than re-testing ones you know.
  • Keep in mind that the answer can repeat a letter.

Strategy notes

Early guesses are for gathering information, not for winning. Cover the common vowels and consonants first, then use positional logic: a yellow letter can never sit in the square where it turned yellow. Doubled letters catch out plenty of people, so consider them when the options look thin. If hard mode is available, it forces you to honor every clue.

Final verdict

Wordle is a near-perfect daily puzzle with elegant rules and huge appeal. The single puzzle per day will leave keen players wanting more. Anyone fond of words should build it into their morning.
